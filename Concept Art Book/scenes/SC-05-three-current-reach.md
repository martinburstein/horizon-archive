## Functional landscape and collective movement

Three landscape-scale systems cross one enormous subsurface reach. They are not pipes, roads, canals, utility corridors, or human workload diagrams enlarged into scenery.

- A **suspended-matter current** moves through a deep porous mineral-ceramic field. Graded pore bodies separate, redistribute, or condition visible particles before the carrier continues.
- A **cyclic-pressure current** moves through broad tensioned ceramic-fiber spans. Alternating compression geometry and anchored diaphragms make periodic pressure physically legible even in a still frame.
- A **conducted-heat current** crosses a jointed refractory substrate. Layered thermal interfaces, expansion joints, oxidation, and local heat refraction make conduction legible without a decorative glow.
- All three remain materially separate through their visible treatment, then enter one broad return bed beneath a continuous glass-ceramic cap and continue beyond the frame. The image proves one visible convergence only; it does not define destination or purpose.

The landscape's beauty comes from a collective movement toward compatibility without sameness. Three older specialist systems retain distinct matter, geometry, and failure behavior. A later reach-wide program introduced compatible junction scales, shared service interfaces, and a common return architecture. Maintained-present repair fabric crosses all three material traditions without flattening them into one design. This is evidence of coordinated work across centuries, not proof of an institution, profession, ideology, author, or intended operator.

Three operating scales must remain visible:

- **Landscape:** the three currents approach from separate horizons and continue through a shared return beyond the crop.
- **Site:** each handling corridor exposes input, transformation, distribution, local bypass, return, and maintenance relationships.
- **Contact:** one low, embedded coupling presents sanitized local sample replicas to the suit while remaining physically isolated from live-current control.

The forms must support at least four live readings—resource conditioning, environmental regulation, waste handling, sensing/communication, preservation, computation, or coupled functions—without choosing one.

## Material, light, atmosphere, and maintenance grammar

- **Porous field:** dense fired mineral ceramic with graded pore sizes, accreted residue, abrasion, replacement plugs, condensation, and believable particle contact. Avoid sponge, coral, mushroom, or fantasy-organic shorthand.
- **Tensioned spans:** ceramic-fiber laminates with anisotropic weave, anchored load paths, stress whitening, fatigue repairs, and credible cyclic deformation. Avoid sails, bridges, strings, harp imagery, or cloth ornament.
- **Thermal bed:** refractory stone-metal composite with expansion interfaces, heat cycling, oxidation, repair laminates, and physically plausible conduction. Avoid lava rivers, magic runes, neon seams, and uniform red glow.
- **Return cap:** thick glass ceramic with variable wall depth, refraction, internal reflection, strain, inclusions, repair fusions, mineral film, and coherent shadows. It reveals convergence geometry without becoming transparent exposition.
- **Shared service grammar:** later compatible collars and repair interfaces repeat across all three systems at different scales, showing collective interoperability rather than cloned parts.
- **Lighting:** geothermal bounce and a distant vault aperture provide the dominant sources. Particles scatter light, tensioned spans catch restrained edge light, and the thermal bed motivates local shimmer. No state adds or changes a light.
- **Atmosphere:** material-specific vapor, particulate density, pressure displacement, and heat refraction establish depth while keeping targets inspectable. Fog, bloom, darkness, or shallow focus cannot conceal weak geometry.
- **Maintenance:** low asymmetric exchange forms temporarily bridge existing service seams and continue on fixed routes. They never resemble people, animals, vehicles, workers, drones, faces, or a response to the player.
