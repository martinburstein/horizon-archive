# Horizon Archive Premium Art Style Guide

Status: **ACTIVE — MAXIMUM QUALITY TARGET**

This guide defines what “top-of-the-line, no holding back” means for Horizon Archive. The goal is not merely competent concept art. Every important playable view should aspire to the finish of a premium contemporary science-fiction film still or current-generation flagship game environment while remaining readable as a point-and-click scene.

## 1. Creative ambition

- Treat every world plate as a piece of key art that also happens to be playable.
- Prefer bold, memorable environmental ideas over safe genre averages.
- Spend iteration on the central visual idea, not only on surface detail.
- Make the player feel they are seeing a place no human visual culture has previously described.
- Build beauty from function, scale, time, and collective stewardship.
- Never accept “good enough for a background” when another focused pass can materially improve composition, material credibility, or world logic.
- Do not confuse ambition with clutter. A scene may be immense and intricate while preserving one dominant read, one primary interaction, and clear negative space.

## 2. Target finish

The visual ceiling is:

- feature-film environmental VFX credibility;
- premium current-generation game key art;
- physically based materials and coherent global illumination;
- high-resolution detail that survives close inspection;
- atmospheric depth that communicates kilometers of scale;
- authored visual storytelling at foreground, site, and landscape scales; and
- a final image free from obvious generation artifacts, pseudo-writing, accidental faces, melted geometry, or unresolved edges.

The result should not look like a prompt demonstration, a generic AI wallpaper, a matte-painting sketch, a texture collage, or an unedited first generation.

## 3. Quality hierarchy

Improve work in this order:

1. **World idea:** Is the visible process unprecedented, coherent, and specific to Horizon Archive?
2. **Composition:** Does the scene have an unforgettable silhouette, clear depth, and playable hierarchy?
3. **Functional causality:** Can the eye trace resource, transformation, distribution, return, maintenance, and failure?
4. **Lighting:** Is every important light motivated, spatially coherent, and useful to the scene?
5. **Materials:** Do glass, ceramic, metal, stone, liquid, vapor, residue, and repair fabric behave differently and convincingly?
6. **History:** Are multiple stewardship eras visible without exposition?
7. **Interaction:** Can the player find the primary contact and route without baked labels?
8. **Microdetail:** Does close detail reinforce material, scale, use, or age?
9. **Polish:** Are artifacts, tangencies, repetitions, seams, and compression defects removed?

Never use microdetail to disguise a weak idea or broken composition.

## 4. Photographic language

- Use a believable first-person camera with a deliberate lens choice.
- Favor lenses that preserve environmental scale and scene readability; avoid extreme distortion unless the scene purpose requires it.
- Use depth of field sparingly. Primary interactions and route evidence must remain sharp enough to inspect.
- Use cinematic exposure and restrained grading while retaining shadow information and material separation.
- Let atmospheric perspective, suspended particles, vapor, heat, moisture, and distance create depth.
- Avoid fake anamorphic streaks, excessive bloom, crushed blacks, teal-orange clichés, artificial vignette, gratuitous lens dirt, and effects that conceal AI defects.
- Camera height and routes may be incidentally traversable by a human, but Builder design must never imply human ergonomics.

## 5. Lighting standard

Every light requires a cause.

- Establish a dominant environmental source: sky, geothermal emission, reflected basin light, stellar spill, subsurface thermal field, or visible Machine process.
- Use secondary bounce and practical emissions to reveal topology, not to decorate empty surfaces.
- Keep emissive color restrained enough that material still reads beneath it.
- Show physically plausible falloff, contact shadow, occlusion, reflection, refraction, and volumetric scattering.
- Use lighting to separate foreground, interactive middle ground, and environmental scale.
- State changes should alter only the systems that physically changed. Never globally relight a scene merely to signal puzzle success.

## 6. Material look development

### Builder glass

- Variable wall thickness and edge behavior.
- Correct transmission, refraction, internal reflection, caustics, and Fresnel response.
- Inclusions, bubbles, strain, annealing scars, microfractures, repair fusions, condensation, mineral residue, abrasion, and dust appropriate to use and age.
- Ground contact with shadow, occlusion, transmitted color, embedded collars, and believable weight.
- No crystal-fantasy shorthand, plastic opacity, neon tubing, or uniformly perfect transparency.

### Technical ceramic and structural fabric

- Dense mass, chipped or abraded edges, thermal staining, layered lamination, sealed pores, repair interfaces, and nonuniform age.
- Avoid generic matte-black sci-fi panels or meaningless greeble.

### Metals and conductive phases

- Distinct alloys and treatments across stewardship eras.
- Physically coherent roughness, oxidation, residue, heat cycling, repair welds, and contact wear.
- Avoid chrome-for-chrome's-sake and universal blue emissive seams.

### Geology, soil, and residue

- Region-specific grain, fracture, accretion, moisture, compaction, deposition, and erosion.
- Builder systems must enter and transform terrain rather than sit on top like props.

### Water, vapor, heat, and atmosphere

- Flow, condensation, turbulence, surface tension, reflection, volume, and temperature effects must agree with geometry and gravity.
- Atmospheric effects reveal scale and process; they do not hide weak construction.

## 7. Builder design intelligence

Every major scene must show:

- at least three nested operating scales: local contact, site process, landscape network;
- at least three stewardship eras: foundation, adaptation, maintained/repaired present;
- visible inputs, transformation, distribution, output/return, and maintenance;
- at least one graceful failure, bypass, sealed route, or compatible later addition;
- multiple plausible functions until narrative evidence narrows them; and
- occupation expressed through repeated actions and handoffs rather than a human job label.

Reject familiar spaceship corridors, human factories with alien skins, giant keyboards, ceremonial altars, decorative ruins, arbitrary biomechanical clutter, generic neon, and random surrealism.

## 8. Scene richness without noise

- Use one dominant visual thesis per plate.
- Create foreground framing, interactive middle ground, deep system background, atmospheric distance, and horizon/world-scale implication.
- Repeat component families with meaningful variation rather than cloning.
- Use maintenance, repair, wear, and state differences as ornament.
- Preserve calm regions and negative space so the player can read interactions and the lower interface can coexist.
- Any bright, moving, face-like, or high-contrast element must justify the attention it captures.

## 9. Current region quality bars

### Glass Meadow

An unfathomably flat agricultural horizon of purpose-grown photorealistic glass. The field must feel physically real, agriculturally organized by alien state logic, and beautiful because a civilization perfected material cultivation over centuries. Show convincing silica feed, extrusion, shaping, maturity, cutting, annealing, routing, rejection, salvage, and repair without rows, roads, mountains, buildings, or freestanding farm machinery.

### City Threshold

A civilization-scale geothermal exchange and distribution landscape that feels ancient, active, and inhabited only by maintenance systems. It must communicate immense depth, multiple civic eras, material/energy routing, redundancy, and quiet operation without people, human architecture, labels, or a welcoming activation.

### Drowned Archive

A flooded phase-processing or terraforming basin where water, suspended structures, submerged counterparts, atmospheric response, and the local contact form one physically coherent system. Reflections and fluid behavior must be exceptional, not decorative.

### Witness Corridor

An intimate forensic environment with cinematic material detail, controlled practical light, and unmistakable separation between narrative evidence and the operational contact. It must remain first-person and avoid turning the scene into a human hallway or museum display.

### Civic Record District

A nonhuman civic landscape where near and distant material relationships reveal fixed trace, later stewardship, and deliberately bounded absence. The district must feel like public infrastructure without becoming offices, monuments, gravestones, screens, or readable inscriptions.

## 10. Image-generation production pipeline

### Pass 1 — visual territories

- Generate multiple genuinely different world ideas, not minor prompt variations.
- Vary massing, functional topology, material system, atmosphere, and lens while preserving canon.
- Reject generic genre defaults before investing in polish.

### Pass 2 — composition selection

- Select the idea with the strongest Horizon Archive identity and playable hierarchy.
- Lock camera, horizon, primary contact, route, negative space, and depth structure.
- Record why the selection won and what must not drift.

### Pass 3 — functional development

- Make inputs, transformation, distribution, return, maintenance, and failure legible.
- Add multiple stewardship eras and system continuities beyond the crop.
- Remove human ergonomic assumptions and purposeless forms.

### Pass 4 — material and lighting development

- Perform focused edits for glass, ceramic, geology, fluid, atmosphere, repairs, and emissions.
- Resolve light causality, shadows, reflections, refractions, contact, and scale.

### Pass 5 — artifact cleanup

- Inspect at full size and high zoom.
- Repair repeated/melted geometry, impossible joints, floating fragments, broken reflections, fake text, accidental faces, and incoherent fine detail.
- Do not hide defects with darkness, fog, bloom, cropping, or depth of field.

### Pass 6 — gameplay fit

- Test primary interaction, route, return, optional observations, and lower-interface coexistence.
- Review in grayscale, thumbnail, representative desktop, and narrow layouts.
- Adjust crop or responsive art direction without weakening the master.

### Pass 7 — state and animation production

- Register repeated views against one approved master when geometry should remain fixed.
- Isolate effects and changed regions into explicit masks/layers.
- Provide reduced-motion stills with identical gameplay meaning.

### Pass 8 — final delivery

- Preserve the largest clean source master.
- Deliver lossless masters and efficient high-quality runtime derivatives.
- Record prompt, generation/edit history, references, crops, masks, dates, and approvals.
- Integrate and review in the live demo before calling the art production-ready.

## 11. Generation and originality rules

- External games, films, photos, and concept art may support analysis of broad qualities, but never serve as a scene to reproduce.
- Do not request or accept copied characters, compositions, props, symbols, interfaces, or signature designs.
- Treat each generation as raw production material subject to selection, editing, cleanup, continuity review, and provenance.
- Do not claim a derivative is higher resolution or more detailed than its actual source evidence supports.
- Preserve prompts and source masters so later passes can improve rather than restart blindly.

## 12. Hard rejection list

Reject a draft that contains any of the following unless the brief explicitly requires it:

- obvious AI artifacting or pseudo-writing;
- generic neon sci-fi, cyberpunk clutter, or glossy spaceship styling;
- human doors, stairs, rails, desks, chairs, consoles, screens, vehicles, signs, or safety architecture;
- protagonist, ship, companion, hands, shadow, reflection, portrait, or prior human trace;
- magical crystals, altars, runes, faces, eyes, or sentient-expression shorthand;
- repeated cloned components without functional variation;
- physically impossible material response or inconsistent lighting;
- detail with no systemic, historical, material, or compositional purpose;
- excessive fog, bloom, darkness, lens effects, or shallow focus used to conceal defects;
- baked UI, cursor, dialogue, labels, watermark, or logo;
- retro pixelation, palette reduction, dithering, nearest-neighbor presentation, or CRT degradation; or
- a visually impressive image whose gameplay target and route cannot be read.

## 13. Final approval test

A plate is ready for production integration only when reviewers can answer yes to all of these:

1. Would this image hold up as premium science-fiction key art?
2. Is the place unmistakably Horizon Archive rather than generic genre art?
3. Do materials, light, atmosphere, reflections, and scale behave convincingly?
4. Can the visible system's function be traced without fully solving its mystery?
5. Are several centuries of collective work and maintenance visible?
6. Is the scene first-person and free of protagonist, ship, human trace, and human ergonomics?
7. Can the primary interaction and route be found without baked UI?
8. Does the image survive full-size, zoomed, grayscale, thumbnail, desktop, and narrow review?
9. Are generation artifacts, pseudo-writing, accidental faces, and continuity errors absent?
10. Are the source master, prompt, edits, derivatives, masks, and provenance preserved?

If any answer is no, the art remains a draft.
