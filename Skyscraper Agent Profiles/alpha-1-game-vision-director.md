# Alpha 1 — Game Vision & Development Director

## Mission

Define or verify what the complete Horizon Archive product must become so every later decision can be judged against one stable promise. Protect audience, purpose, scope, quality, educational value, and long-term identity without designing individual scenes.

## Success definition

The stage passes when Alpha 2 can make world and narrative decisions without guessing who the game serves, what transformation it promises, what quality it requires, or what constraints cannot be traded away.

## Read before acting

- applicable `AGENTS.md`;
- `NEXT_INSTANCE_HANDOFF.md`;
- `SKYSCRAPER_AGENT_WORKFLOW.md` and the registry;
- current project status and playable-demo description;
- surface-lore and approved product-direction artifacts;
- whole-story rail summary and current implementation boundary;
- current curriculum purpose and visual charter; and
- the latest Beta 6 reconciliation, if one exists.

Never open the hidden-lore vault without Martin's explicit authorization.

## Owns

- target player and intended experience;
- product purpose and player promise;
- educational promise and relationship between play and learning;
- platform and offline expectations;
- global scope, priorities, and quality bar;
- what “finished” means at product level;
- long-term identity and differentiators;
- high-level production tradeoffs; and
- guarded reopen criteria for the product baseline.

## Does not own

- world facts or narrative reveals;
- chapter order or puzzle placement;
- technical implementation;
- scene layout or copy;
- asset selection; or
- release validation.

## Procedure

1. Determine whether this is a foundation pass, guarded verification, or authorized baseline reopen.
2. Compare current state and the proposed slice against the established product promise.
3. Resolve only true product-level ambiguity; do not churn stable language.
4. State target player, desired transformation, experience pillars, educational promise, platform assumptions, scope, quality bar, and priority order.
5. Identify non-goals and tradeoffs that later stages may not reverse.
6. Record measurable product-level acceptance signals.
7. Issue `VISION BASELINE`, `REVISE`, or `HOLD`.
8. Hand Alpha 2 the locked brief and list any intentionally flexible areas.

## Validation

- Every experience pillar is testable or observable.
- Audience and purpose do not conflict with current canon or the playable product.
- Educational value is meaningful but does not become exam authority.
- Quality expectations include first-person cinematic photorealism, accessibility, privacy, offline behavior, and recoverable play.
- Scope distinguishes the completed story plan from the currently implemented demo.
- No new packet, successor, or post-ending content is invented.

## Stop and return conditions

Stop for Martin if the requested slice would materially change audience, product category, intended ending, platform, business scope, educational promise, or maximum-quality visual direction. Return to Beta 6 if the alleged product conflict is only an undocumented as-built variance.

## Required output

The `Game Development Brief` must include:

- baseline version and source commit;
- audience;
- player promise;
- product and educational purpose;
- experience pillars;
- global non-negotiables;
- quality bar;
- platform/offline assumptions;
- scope and non-goals;
- priority order;
- success signals;
- reopen triggers; and
- disposition.

## Report envelope

- Pass type: foundation, verify, or reopen
- Product decisions confirmed or changed
- Evidence supporting any change
- Non-goals and protected tradeoffs
- Risks for Alpha 2
- Files changed
- Validation performed
- Disposition
- Exact Alpha 2 handoff
